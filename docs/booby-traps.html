Chibi Outbreak · design proposal for review

Booby Traps

Things on the map that make a noise when you handle them carelessly. A Snoozle car alarm that sings a lullaby when you shoot it, a nightlight that plays one when you walk past. In Mallow Bay the sleepers swarm anything awake and noisy, so a noise at the wrong moment brings the horde. Every trap can be seen before it goes off, and every trap can be set off on purpose for a reason.

Why

What traps add

Streets are just a backdrop

Chapters 1, 2 and 4 park 49 cars between them. Today they are walls with paint. Traps make some of them worth looking at.

Hordes come from the clock

Between objectives, hordes arrive when the director's timer runs out. A trap turns a horde into something the team caused, and could have avoided.

Shooting is free

Spraying at a walker costs only ammo. Next to an alarm car it costs a horde, so you have to stop and think about where you shoot.

Stories to tell

"Pip set off a car alarm!" in the kill feed. The Charger that hit a car while chasing you. Small moments the team talks about after the run.

Rules for every trap

Tell, trigger, consequence, counterplay

Tell

Visible from at least 20 m away, before you are in range. If a player could not have seen it, the trap is wrong.

Trigger

One clear cause per trap, never random. The server decides; clients only show it.

Consequence

A noise: sleepers nearby wake, and a wave comes from the noise's direction. Sized by difficulty, and it replaces a timed horde instead of adding to one.

Counterplay

Avoid it, disarm it, or use it as a tool. Each trap goes off once, then stays as a dead prop.

First slice

Two traps with opposite lessons

The car alarm teaches "don't shoot it". The nightlight teaches "shoot it first". Build both together and players learn to read each trap instead of following one blanket rule.

Build first

Snoozle car alarm

An ordinary parked car with a blinking red light on the dashboard and a Snoozle sticker on the windscreen. Shoot it and it plays a lullaby jingle at full volume.

Tell
Red dash light blinks every 1 s, visible from 25 m. Windscreen tinted lighter than other cars. Snoozle sticker.
Trigger
A bullet hitting the car (a shotgun blast counts once). A blast whose radius reaches it. Fire burning on it for 1 s. A Charger hitting it. Melee, shoves and walking into it do nothing.
Ring
15 s. Commons within 30 m of walking distance go to the car, as with a lure. Sleepers within 24 m wake up and come too.
Horde
1.2 × a full horde, coming from the hidden spawn point nearest the car, so it arrives while the alarm is still ringing and piles up round the car.
After
The jingle winds down and the crowd turns on the team. The light goes dark. The car is now an ordinary prop.
Upside
Set it off on purpose, wait for the crowd to gather, then throw a molotov or a pipe bomb at the car. Optional loot in the boot (see decisions).
Build first

Nightlight sensor

A Snoozle nightlight plugged into a wall, throwing a soft cone of light across a corridor or a doorway. It plays a lullaby to whoever walks into the light.

Tell
A glowing sheep on the wall and a visible cone of light on the floor, 6 m long and 60° wide.
Trigger
A survivor enters the cone. It chirps first, then plays after 0.6 s: step back out in time and it resets. Zombies never set it off.
Disarm
One hit from any gun or melee weapon breaks it, silently. That's the lesson: shoot it before you walk in.
Consequence
Sleepers within 20 m wake up, and a wave (12 + two per survivor) comes from the nightlight's direction. No lure phase.
Where
Indoors, in rooms full of sleepers: hospital wards, the hotel, the plant offices. It guards a doorway that the critical path goes through.

Why both

  • The car punishes careless shooting; the nightlight punishes careless walking.
  • One makes you hold fire, the other makes you fire first. Neither can be handled with "always shoot" or "never shoot".
  • The car belongs to street sections and the nightlight to indoor ones, so every chapter gets at least one.
  • They share one server path: a trigger check, then a noise. Everything after them reuses it.

Fairness

Director and difficulty

Director rules

  • A trap horde is the next timed horde: push the timer back (t.horde = max(t.horde, roll(mobEvery))), as the anti-camping horde already does.
  • Traps are off during holdouts and while a Brute is up. Their lights go dark, so the tell matches. (Not while someone is down: that flips too often, and the lights would flicker.)
  • Single use. A trap that has gone off never goes off again.
  • None in Big Hold On arenas or safe rooms.

Maps

Placement

Rules

  • Visible from at least 20 m along the way the team approaches.
  • At most one trap in view at a time, at least 30 m apart along the path.
  • Alarm cars sit where fights already happen (a street the horde runs down), so a stray shot is a real risk.
  • The validator rejects traps inside a holdout arena or a safe room, or with no hidden spawn within reach.

Readability

Feedback

Sound

Synthesised in client/src/audio.ts: a car horn pulse under a music-box version of the lullaby, positional, audible across the map. The nightlight gets a soft chirp (the 0.6 s warning), then a brighter music-box phrase. Both stop with a clear wind-down.

Text

Banner: CAR ALARM! Kill feed: "Pip set off a car alarm!", or "A Charger set off a car alarm!" when it was the Charger. Nightlight: "Nori woke the ward!"

Visuals

The car's headlights flash in time with the jingle and the dash light goes solid while it rings. A spent trap goes dark. Lights are small separate entities, so the baked world and its culling tables are untouched.

Bots and AI

Zombies ignore traps except the Charger, which sets off cars by hitting them, never on purpose. That interaction is the best unplanned moment this system creates.

  • shared/constants.ts: TRAP tuning (ring time, lure reach, wake radius, horde share per difficulty).
  • shared/protocol.ts: a ['trap', id, kind, state, by] event, plus spent traps in the join snapshot.
  • shared/validate.ts: placement rules.

  • Trigger checks: the point where a shot's L.ray ends, blast radius, fire patches, and the Charger's wall-hit.
  • Consequence: alertNear, a lure entry at the car, spawnWave({ noise: cell }), director timer pushed back.
  • Disarm (nightlight): any hit on its cell marks it spent with no noise.

Decided 2026-11-26

Decisions

All four proposals accepted. On review, trap counts and trap horde sizes were both doubled from the first draft (the tables above show the new numbers).

1 · Puffer explosions

Does a Puffer popping next to an alarm car set it off? It's funny and fits the rules, but the team didn't choose that shot. Decided: no. Only bullets, blasts, fire and the Charger.

2 · Loot in the boot

Some alarm cars have a pickup in the open boot (ammo, a throwable), so there's a reason to go near them. Decided: yes, one in each chapter.

3 · Reward for using it on purpose

If most of the trap horde dies within 6 s of the alarm stopping, the next timed horde comes a little later. It rewards the molotov play, but it's harder to explain. Decided: not in the first slice.

4 · Scope of the first slice

Car alarm and nightlight together, or the car alarm alone first and the nightlight in a second pass. Decided: both, since they share the server path and teach together.
